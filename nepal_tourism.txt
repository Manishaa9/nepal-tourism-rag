Nepal is a beautiful country located in South Asia,
famous for its Himalayan mountains, rich culture, and friendly
people. The capital city is Kathmandu.

Kathmandu Valley:
Kathmandu is known for its ancient temples and UNESCO World
Heritage Sites. Key places to visit include Kathmandu Durbar
Square, Patan Durbar Square, Bhaktapur Durbar Square, Swayambhunath 
(Monkey Temple), and Pashupatinath Temple. The best time to visit is
during the spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November).

Pokhara:
Pokhara is the second-largest city in Nepal and the gateway to the
Annapurna region. It is famous for Phewa Lake, where tourists can go
boating. Other attractions include Davis Falls, Gupteshwor Cave, and 
the World Peace Pagoda. Pokhara is also the starting point for the 
Annapurna Base Camp trek.

Everest Region:
The Everest region is home to Mount Everest, the highest mountain in the
world. The most popular activity is the Everest Base Camp trek, which usually
 takes 12 to 14 days. Trekkers fly into Lukla Airport to begin the journey. 
 The best seasons for this trek are spring and autumn.

Trekking Permits:
To trek in most regions of Nepal, tourists need a TIMS (Trekkers' Information
Management System) card and a National Park entry permit. These can be
obtained in Kathmandu or Pokhara. For the Everest region, a Sagarmatha 
National Park permit is required.

Visa Information:
Most foreign tourists can obtain a visa on arrival at Tribhuvan International
 Airport in Kathmandu. A 30-day visa costs around USD 50. You will need a 
 passport valid for at least six months and a passport-sized photo.

 Popular Nepali Food:
Dal Bhat is the national dish of Nepal, consisting of steamed rice, lentil soup, 
and vegetables. Momo are Nepali dumplings usually filled with buffalo, chicken, 
or vegetables, served with a spicy tomato chutney. Sel Roti is a traditional sweet,
 ring-shaped rice bread.

Cultural Etiquette in Nepal:
When greeting someone, use the word "Namaste" with your palms pressed together. Always 
remove your shoes before entering a temple or someone's home. Do not touch someone's 
head, as it is considered the most sacred part of the body. Public displays of affection 
are generally frowned upon.

Adventure Activities:
Chitwan National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for jungle safaris, where 
you can see one-horned rhinos and Bengal tigers. Pokhara is one of the best places in 
the world for paragliding, offering stunning views of the Annapurna mountains. Trishuli 
River is the most popular spot for white-water rafting.


Major Festivals in Nepal:
Dashain is the biggest and longest festival in Nepal, celebrated for 15 days, usually 
in October. It celebrates the victory of good over evil. Tihar is the festival of lights, 
celebrated shortly after Dashain, where people worship crows, dogs, cows, and brothers. 
Holi is the festival of colors, celebrated in March, where people throw colored powder 
and water at each other. Indra Jatra is a major festival in Kathmandu featuring masked 
dances and the living goddess Kumari.

Lumbini:
Lumbini is a Buddhist pilgrimage site in the Rupandehi District of Nepal. It is the 
birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, who later became the Buddha. It is a UNESCO World 
Heritage Site and features the Maya Devi Temple, sacred gardens, and monasteries built 
by various countries.

Langtang Valley Trek:
The Langtang Valley trek is a popular alternative to the Everest and Annapurna treks. 
It is closer to Kathmandu and takes about 7 to 10 days. The trail passes through 
beautiful forests, traditional Tamang villages, and offers spectacular mountain views. 
It is less crowded than the Everest region.

Currency and Money:
The official currency of Nepal is the Nepalese Rupee (NPR). ATMs are widely available 
in Kathmandu, Pokhara, and other major cities. However, it is highly recommended to 
carry cash if you are trekking in remote areas, as ATMs are rare and often unreliable. 
Credit cards are accepted in most hotels and restaurants in tourist areas.

Internet and Connectivity:
Wi-Fi is widely available in hotels, cafes, and restaurants in Kathmandu and Pokhara. 
For remote trekking areas, you can buy a local SIM card from Ncell or Nepal Telecom (NTC) 
for better coverage, though signal strength can be limited in high mountain regions. 
Everest Base Camp now has limited Wi-Fi access for a fee.

Best Time to Visit Nepal:
The best time to visit Nepal is during the autumn season (September to November) and the 
spring season (March to May). During these months, the weather is clear, the skies are blue, 
and the mountain views are spectacular. The monsoon season (June to August) brings heavy rain,
landslides, and leeches on the trails. Winter (December to February) is very cold in the
 mountains, though trekking in lower elevations is still possible.

 Getting Around Nepal:
Domestic flights connect Kathmandu to major cities like Pokhara, Biratnagar, and Lukla. 
Tourist buses are a popular and affordable way to travel between cities like Kathmandu, 
Pokhara, and Chitwan. Taxis are available in cities, but you should always agree on a 
price before getting in. Local buses are very cheap but can be crowded and slow. Renting
a motorcycle is possible in Pokhara and Kathmandu if you have a valid international 
driving permit.

Accommodation in Nepal:
In cities like Kathmandu and Pokhara, you can find luxury hotels, boutique guesthouses,
and budget hostels. While trekking, you will stay in "teahouses," which are basic mountain
 lodges offering simple rooms and meals. Homestays are a great way to experience traditional 
 rural life and are becoming increasingly popular in villages like Ghandruk and Bandipur.

Health and Safety:
Altitude sickness (AMS) is a real risk when trekking above 2,500 meters. Symptoms include 
headache, nausea, and dizziness. The best prevention is to ascend slowly and stay hydrated. 
Do not drink tap water; always drink bottled or purified water. Travel insurance that covers 
high-altitude trekking and helicopter evacuation is highly recommended.

Shopping in Nepal:
Thamel in Kathmandu is the main shopping district for tourists. Popular souvenirs include 
Pashmina shawls, Tibetan carpets, Thangka paintings (traditional Buddhist scroll paintings), 
and handmade jewelry. Bargaining is expected in tourist shops, so don't accept the first price. 

Geography of Nepal:
Nepal is divided into three main geographical regions: the Himalayan region (high mountains 
and snow), the Hilly region (green hills, valleys, and terraces), and the Terai region 
(flat plains, forests, and wildlife parks). Mount Everest is in the Himalayan region, 
while Chitwan National Park is in the Terai.

Packing Tips for Trekking:
Bring layered clothing, a good quality down jacket, sturdy hiking boots, a sleeping bag, 
a headlamp, sunscreen, and a reusable water bottle. A good trekking map and a basic first-aid 
kit are also essential. 

Travel Tips:
Always carry a copy of your passport and visa. Keep some small change in Nepalese Rupees for
daily expenses. Tipping is appreciated but not mandatory in restaurants and by trekking guides.
Respect local customs by dressing modestly and asking before taking photos of people or religious
sites.

--- 1. OVERVIEW AND GEOGRAPHY ---
Nepal is a landlocked country in South Asia, bordered by China (Tibet) to the north and India to the south, east, and west. It is famous for its Himalayan mountains, rich cultural heritage, and biodiversity. The capital city is Kathmandu.

Nepal is divided into three main geographical regions:
*   The Himalayan Region: Home to the world's highest peaks, including Mount Everest (8,848.86 meters). This region features snow-capped mountains, glaciers, and high-altitude valleys.
*   The Hilly Region: Characterized by green hills, terraced farmlands, and river valleys. This region includes cities like Pokhara and the Kathmandu Valley.
*   The Terai Region: A flat, fertile plain in the south that borders India. It contains dense forests, national parks, and agricultural land. This is where Chitwan National Park is located.

--- 2. WORLD HERITAGE SITES IN NEPAL ---
Nepal has four UNESCO World Heritage Sites: two cultural and two natural.
*   Cultural Sites:
    *   Kathmandu Valley: This valley contains seven distinct monument zones, including the Durbar Squares of Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur, as well as the Hindu temples of Pashupatinath and Changu Narayan, and the Buddhist stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath.
    *   Lumbini: The birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, who later became the Lord Buddha. It is a major pilgrimage site featuring the Maya Devi Temple and numerous monasteries built by different countries.
*   Natural Sites:
    *   Chitwan National Park: Established in 1973, it is Nepal's first national park. It is famous for its jungle safaris where visitors can see one-horned rhinos, Bengal tigers, crocodiles, and various bird species.
    *   Sagarmatha National Park: This park encompasses the Everest region and is home to the highest peak in the world. It is known for its dramatic mountain landscapes, Sherpa culture, and unique flora and fauna.

--- 3. MAJOR DESTINATIONS AND CITIES ---

**Kathmandu Valley:**
The cultural heart of Nepal. Key attractions include:
*   Kathmandu Durbar Square: A historic royal palace complex with ancient temples and courtyards.
*   Patan Durbar Square: Known for its artistic heritage and the Patan Museum.
*   Bhaktapur Durbar Square: A well-preserved medieval city known for its pottery and woodcarving.
*   Swayambhunath (Monkey Temple): An ancient Buddhist stupa offering panoramic views of the valley.
*   Pashupatinath Temple: Nepal's most sacred Hindu temple, located on the banks of the Bagmati River.
*   Boudhanath Stupa: One of the largest Buddhist stupas in the world and a center of Tibetan culture in Nepal.

**Pokhara:**
The second-largest city and the gateway to the Annapurna region. It is famous for:
*   Phewa Lake: A serene lake where visitors can go boating and enjoy views of the Annapurna mountains.
*   Davis Falls and Gupteshwor Cave: A unique waterfall that disappears into a underground cave.
*   World Peace Pagoda: A Buddhist monument offering stunning views of the city and mountains.
*   Adventure Activities: Pokhara is a hub for paragliding, zip-lining, and ultra-light flights.

**Lumbini:**
A UNESCO World Heritage Site and the birthplace of the Buddha. It is a major pilgrimage destination featuring the Maya Devi Temple, sacred gardens, and monasteries from various countries.

**Chitwan National Park:**
A UNESCO World Heritage Site famous for its jungle safaris, where you can see one-horned rhinos and Bengal tigers. Activities include canoeing, jungle walks, and visiting local Tharu villages.

--- 4. TREKKING IN NEPAL ---
Nepal is a world-famous trekking destination. The best seasons are Spring (March to May) and Autumn (September to November).

*   Everest Base Camp Trek: The most iconic trek. It starts with a flight to Lukla and passes through Namche Bazaar, Tengboche, and Gorak Shep before reaching the base camp at 5,364 meters. Duration: 12-16 days.
*   Annapurna Circuit Trek: A legendary trek that circles the Annapurna mountain range and crosses the Thorong La Pass (5,416 meters). Duration: 15-20 days.
*   Annapurna Base Camp Trek: A shorter trek that takes you into the heart of the Annapurna sanctuary. Duration: 7-10 days.
*   Langtang Valley Trek: A relatively short trek close to Kathmandu, passing through Tamang villages and beautiful forests. Duration: 7-10 days.
*   Manaslu Circuit Trek: A restricted-area trek around Mount Manaslu, offering pristine nature and Tibetan-influenced culture. Duration: 14-18 days.

Trekking Permits: Most treks require a TIMS (Trekkers' Information Management System) card and a National Park entry permit. These can be obtained in Kathmandu or Pokhara.

--- 3. CULTURE, ETHNIC GROUPS, AND TRADITIONS ---
Nepal is a multi-ethnic, multi-lingual, and multi-cultural country. The 2021 census identified over 125 ethnic groups and 123 languages.

**Major Ethnic Groups:**
- Chhetri: Historically the ruling and warrior class.
- Brahmin (Bahun): Traditionally priests and scholars.
- Magar: An indigenous group, mainly in the western hills.
- Tharu: An indigenous group of the Terai region.
- Tamang: An indigenous group of the central hills.
- Newar: The indigenous people of the Kathmandu Valley, known for their rich art, architecture, and festivals.
- Kami: Traditionally blacksmiths.
- Yadav: An Indo-Aryan group in the Terai.
- Rai: An indigenous group of the eastern hills.
- Gurung: An indigenous group of the western hills, known for their bravery (Gurkhas).

**Languages:**
The official language is Nepali, written in the Devanagari script. Other major languages include Maithili, Bhojpuri, Tharu, Tamang, Newari (Nepal Bhasa), and Magar.

**Religions:**
- Hinduism: Practiced by about 81% of the population.
- Buddhism: Practiced by about 9% of the population.
- Islam: Practiced by about 4.4% of the population.
- Kirat: Practiced by about 3% of the population.
- Christianity: Practiced by about 1.4% of the population.

**Cultural Traditions:**
- Greeting: "Namaste" with palms pressed together.
- Tika: A mixture of yogurt, rice, and red powder applied to the forehead as a blessing.
- Dress: Daura-Suruwal for men and Gunyu-Cholo for women are traditional outfits.
- Dance and Music: Diverse styles like Maruni, Chudka, and Dohori. Traditional instruments include the Madal and Sarangi.

--- 4. MAJOR FESTIVALS ---
- Dashain: The biggest and longest festival, celebrating the victory of good over evil. Celebrated for 15 days in September/October.
- Tihar: The festival of lights, celebrated in October/November. People worship crows, dogs, cows, and brothers.
- Holi: The festival of colors, celebrated in February/March.
- Indra Jatra: A major festival in Kathmandu featuring masked dances and the living goddess Kumari.
- Chhath: A major festival in the Terai region, dedicated to the Sun God.
- Losar: The Tibetan New Year, celebrated by the Sherpa and Tamang communities.
- Buddha Jayanti: Celebrates the birth, enlightenment, and death of Lord Buddha.
- Maghe Sankranti: Marks the beginning of the month of Magh and the end of the winter solstice.

- Connectivity: Wi-Fi is widely available. Local SIM cards (Ncell, NTC) are recommended for remote areas.
- Shopping: Thamel in Kathmandu is the main shopping district for souvenirs like Pashmina shawls, carpets, and Thangka paintings.

=== NEPAL TOURISM: DETAILED INFORMATION EXTENSION ===

--- 1. DETAILED TREKKING ROUTES ---
Nepal is the ultimate destination for trekkers. One in every five visitors comes solely for trekking. The popular seasons are Spring (February-May) and Autumn (September-November). During the monsoon (June-August), you can trek in rain-shadow areas like Mustang, Manang, and Dolpa where the rain clouds cannot reach.

**Everest Base Camp Trek (Everest Region):**
The Everest region is located in the northeast of Nepal. The trek begins at Lukla, the airport at 2,850 meters. The trail climbs up the Dudhkoshi River Valley. The prime attraction is Mount Everest (8,848 meters), which lies in Sagarmatha National Park. The park is also home to Lhotse and Cho Oyu, besides several other peaks above 6,000 meters. Designated a World Heritage Site in 1979, much of the 1,148 sq. km park lies above 3,000 meters. The park is home to the ghoral, tahr, serow, and musk deer, as well as the impeyan pheasant, blood pheasant, red-billed chough, and yellow-billed chough. Duration: 12-14 days. Difficulty: Strenuous altitude trek. Highest point: Kala Patthar ~5,545 m.

**Annapurna Circuit Trek (Annapurna Region):**
A legendary trek that circles the Annapurna mountain range and crosses the Thorong La Pass (5,416 meters). Duration: 14-21 days. Difficulty: Moderate to Hard. The best seasons are October-November and March-April.

**Annapurna Base Camp Trek:**
A shorter trek that takes you into the heart of the Annapurna sanctuary. Duration: 7-10 days. It is one of the most popular treks and offers beginner-friendly options.

**Manaslu Circuit Trek (Manaslu Region):**
A restricted-area trek around Mount Manaslu, offering pristine nature and Tibetan-influenced culture. The trek crosses the Larkya La Pass (5,106 meters). Duration: 14-18 days. Difficulty: Hard. It is a remote, wilder restricted-area route with fewer crowds.

**Langtang Valley Trek (Langtang Region):**
A relatively short trek close to Kathmandu, passing through Tamang villages and beautiful forests. Langtang National Park was established in 1976 and covers an area of 1,710 sq. km. It is a high mountain park home to musk deer and other wildlife. Duration: 7-10 days. It offers authentic experiences with fewer crowds.

**Gokyo Lakes Trek (Everest Region):**
A stunning trek that takes you to the turquoise Gokyo Lakes. You can climb Gokyo Ri for unforgettable sunrise views of Everest, Lhotse, Makalu, and the lakes. Duration: 10-12 days.

**Upper Mustang Trek:**
A restricted-area trek in the rain-shadow area of Mustang. It offers arid landscapes, Tibetan culture, and the pilgrimage site of Muktinath. Popular for road trips and trekking.

**Other Trekking Areas:**
- Gorkha and Manaslu: The East Trek and The West Trek.
- Khaptad: A trek in the far western region.
- The Karnali Trek: A remote trek in the western region.
- Dolpa: A remote and restricted area trek.
- Mugu: A remote trek in the Karnali region.
- Humla: A remote trek in the far northwest.
- Trekking around Palpa: A lesser-known trekking area.
- Trekking in and around Kathmandu Valley: Nagarkot, Daman, Kakani, Shivapuri.
- Heritage Trails: Limbu Cultural Trail, Tamang Heritage Trail, Chitwan Chepang Hill Trail, Pikey-Dudhkunda Cultural Trail (Solu), Gurung Heritage Trail, Dolpa Heritage Circuit, Annapurna Dhaulagiri Community Eco-Trek.
- The Great Himalaya Trail: A long-distance trek across the entire Himalaya.

--- 2. NATIONAL PARKS OF NEPAL ---
Nepal is home to 12 national parks, 1 wildlife reserve, and 1 hunting reserve. These protected areas are home to some of the most endangered animals in the world, including the greater one-horned rhinoceros, Bengal tiger, Asian elephant, gharial, and pangolin.

- Chitwan National Park: Established in 1973, it is Nepal's first national park. It is a UNESCO World Heritage Site (1984) and covers 367.81 square miles of the Inner Terai lowlands of south-central Nepal. The park has subtropical climate with high humidity. It contains broadleaf forests covering 70% of the area and grasslands covering 20%. The grasslands have some of the tallest grasses in the world, such as elephant grass. The park sustains more than 700 species of wildlife, including mugger crocodiles, gharials, Bengal tigers, Indian leopards, sloth bears, and Indian rhinoceros. The Alluvial floodplain of the Terai is thought to be one of the best tiger habitats in the world.
- Sagarmatha National Park: A UNESCO World Heritage Site established in 1976, covering 1,148 sq. km. It is home to Mount Everest and other high peaks. The park is composed of rugged terrain with deep gorges, glaciers, and huge rocks. Vegetation varies from temperate plants like pine, hemlock, fir, juniper, birch, and rhododendron to alpine plants and then to bare rock and snow. It is home to the ghoral, tahr, serow, and musk deer, as well as the impeyan pheasant, blood pheasant, red-billed chough, and yellow-billed chough.
- Langtang National Park: Established in 1976, covering 1,710 sq. km. It is a high mountain park home to musk deer and other wildlife.
- Bardiya National Park: Established in 1988. It is famous for Bengal tigers and wild elephants. One of the most famous elephants, Raja Gaj, measured an awe-inspiring 11.3 ft tall at the shoulder.
- Shivapuri Nagarjun National Park: Located near Kathmandu.
- Makalu Barun National Park: Home to Mount Makalu.
- Rara National Park: Home to Rara Lake, the largest lake in Nepal.
- Khaptad National Park: A national park in the far western region.
- Shuklaphanta National Park: A national park in the far western Terai.
- Shey Phoksundo National Park: Home to Phoksundo Lake.
- Banke National Park: A national park in the western Terai.
- Parsa National Park: A national park in the central Terai.

--- 3. ADVENTURE ACTIVITIES IN NEPAL ---
Nepal's outdoor adventure portfolio covers 8 distinct activity categories: trekking (14 designated regions), mountaineering (27 peaks open for permit climbing), white-water rafting (6 rated rivers), paragliding, bungee jumping, zip-lining, mountain biking, and rock climbing.

**Paragliding in Pokhara:**
Glide above Phewa Lake with panoramic Himalayan views. Pokhara is Asia's premier paragliding site. Approximate cost: USD 80-120.

**Bungee Jumping:**
Take the leap from one of Nepal's highest bungee platforms. The Kushma bungee jump is the second-highest in the world. Approximate cost: USD 80-100.

**White Water Rafting:**
Navigate thrilling rapids on the Trishuli, Bhote Koshi, or Seti Rivers while enjoying spectacular mountain scenery. The Trishuli River provides a half-day rafting option 2 hours from Kathmandu. Approximate cost: USD 25-70.

**Zipline Adventure:**
Fly at high speed over forests, rivers, and deep valleys with incredible Himalayan backdrops. Approximate cost: USD 30-60.

**Mountain Flight:**
Enjoy a scenic flight offering breathtaking aerial views of Everest and the Himalayan range. Ideal for families and non-trekkers. Approximate cost: USD 150-250.

**Everest Helicopter Tour:**
Fly over the world's highest mountains and enjoy a close-up view of Mount Everest without trekking. Perfect for photographers and travelers short on time. Approximate cost: USD 1,100-1,500.

**Rock Climbing:**
Popular near Hattiban and Nagarjun.

**Mountain Biking:**
A popular activity in the Kathmandu Valley and around Pokhara.

**Canyoning:**
Available in Rumsi Waterfall and other locations.

**Ultra-light Flights:**
Available in Pokhara.

**Sunrise and Sunset Viewpoints:**
- Nagarkot: The closest panoramic viewpoint from Kathmandu, famous for sunrise over the Himalayas.

--- 4. NEPALI CUISINE ---
A typical Nepalese meal is dal-bhat-tarkari. Dal is a spicy lentil soup, served over bhat (boiled rice), served with tarkari (curried vegetables) together with achar (pickles) or chutni (spicy condiment made from fresh ingredients).

**Newari Cuisine:**
The Newar community of the Kathmandu Valley has a rich culinary tradition. Famous Newari dishes include:
- Samay Baji: An authentic traditional dish of the Newar community. It is a platter that consists of many items on a single plate. The main components are flattened rice (chiura), fried black soybeans (bhatmas), lentil pancakes (wa/bara), rice pancakes (chatamari), buffalo meat (chhwela), finely cut ginger (palu), fried boiled egg (khen), fried fish (sanyaa), pickled boiled beans (bodi ko achar), spicy potato salad (Aalu-Wala), greens (saag), and Newar wine (Ayla). Samay baji has achar which is a tangy and spicy medley of different vegetables: mostly radish, carrot, onion, potatoes, and peas, amalgamated with a unique Nepalese berry called lapsi. Samay baji is eaten as a starter in every festival, every religious activity, and pujas.
- Choila: A typical Newari dish that consists of spiced grilled buffalo meat. Nowadays mutton, chicken, duck meat, and mushroom are also used.
- Chatamari: Also known as Newari pizza, this is a savory rice flour pancake that is topped with egg, beans, corn, mushrooms, onions, and keema (ground meat).
- Yomari: A steamed sweet bread made of rice flour dough, shaped like a fig with ends like a fishtail, and filled with chaku (a sweet made of sugarcane, ghee, and other ingredients).
- Kwati: A mixed bean soup.
- Bara: Lentil pancakes.
- Sel Roti: A traditional sweet, ring-shaped rice bread.
- Dhindo: A thick porridge made from buckwheat or millet flour.
- Khurma: A sweet made from flour, ghee, and sugar.
- Lakhamari: A hard, sweet biscuit-like snack.
- Aloo Tama: A tangy potato and bamboo shoot curry.
- Chyang: A milky rice beer that tastes faintly like cider.
- Ayla: A traditional Newar wine.

**Momo:**
Nepali dumplings usually filled with buffalo, chicken, or vegetables, served with a spicy tomato chutney.

--- 5. LESSER-KNOWN DESTINATIONS AND HIDDEN GEMS ---

**Rupakot (Khotang):**
A stunning natural and culturally rich tourist destination in Khotang. Located at the top of a hill near the district headquarters of Diktel, at an elevation of 2,600 meters. It offers breathtaking panoramic views, including sightings of Mount Everest, Cho Oyu, Lhotse, Makalu, and Ama Dablam. The area is home to wide-open fields and dense forests, offering opportunities for both short and long treks. Various species of medicinal plants and endangered wildlife can be found here. The area also holds significant historical and religious value, with a stone cave known as Jogi Kuti and Shiddhathan, a revered place of worship for the Rai community. It is situated approximately five meters away from the Mid-Hill Highway. Visitors can access it on foot from Mude and Hurlung.

**Begnas and Rupa Lakes (Pokhara):**
Step beyond Pokhara's crowded lakeside to the quiet waters of Begnas and Rupa, where wildlife, village life, and Himalayan views reveal a deeper Nepal. A short walk from Begnas brings travellers to Rupa Lake, a smaller body of water split by a dramatic suspension bridge.

**Terai Lowlands:**
The Himalayas and Kathmandu may lure all the tourists, but Nepal's Terai lowlands are teeming with wildlife, national parks, and an innovative homestay network. The Terai's Chitwan National Park is a UNESCO World Heritage Site. This region offers a different perspective on Nepal, away from the mountains.

**Dadhiphuk Cave (Upper Mustang):**
Located at Charang in Lho-Ghekar Damodar Kunda Rural Municipality-1 of Upper Mustang. This mysterious cave is attractive and unusual enough to fascinate tourists, but it is still unknown to many.

**Rumsi Village (Tanahu):**
An ethnic Magar-majority community in Rusmi village of Vyas Municipality-14 in Tanahu district. The village has started gaining an identity as a tourist destination. The Bandipur-Rumsi Village tour, the Rumsi Eco Trek, canyoning in Rumsi Waterfall, farmer field schools, cultural exchange programmes, traditional local cuisine experiences, and religious events have been introduced.

**Thakur Patan (Rukum East):**
A high-altitude pastureland (locally called patan) nestled at 3,300 meters in Putha Uttarganga Rural Municipality-5, Rukum East. It sits like a natural amphitheatre, surrounded by towering hills.

**Bagar (Nepal's Secret Lush Gem):**
A Nepalese gem that isn't packed with tourists, but offers next-level green vibes and chill energy for explorers.

**Lwang Village (Kaski):**
An emerging tourism destination in the Kaski district.

**Kalinchowk (Dolakha):**
A high-altitude viewpoint known for spectacular sunrise and sunset above the clouds.

--- 6. UNESCO WORLD HERITAGE SITES IN NEPAL ---
There are four UNESCO World Heritage Sites in Nepal: two cultural and two natural.

**Cultural Sites:**
- Kathmandu Valley: This valley contains seven distinct monument zones, including the Durbar Squares of Kathmandu, Patan, and Bhaktapur, as well as the Hindu temples of Pashupatinath and Changu Narayan, and the Buddhist stupas of Swayambhunath and Boudhanath. These seven monuments are together counted as one Heritage Site.
- Lumbini: The birthplace of Siddhartha Gautama, who later became the Lord Buddha. It was added to the list in 1997.

**Natural Sites:**
- Chitwan National Park: Listed in 1984.
- Sagarmatha National Park: Listed in 1979.

--- 7. MAJOR MOUNTAIN PEAKS OF NEPAL ---
Nepal is home to eight of the world's fourteen eight-thousanders (peaks above 8,000 meters).

- Mount Everest (Sagarmatha): 8,848 meters. Located in Solukhumbu.
- Kangchenjunga: 8,586 meters. Located in Taplejung.
- Lhotse: 8,516 meters. Located in the Khumbu region.
- Yalung Kang: 8,505 meters. Located in Taplejung.
- Makalu: 8,463 meters. Located in Sankhuwasabha.
- Cho Oyu: 8,201 meters. Located in the Khumbu region.
- Dhaulagiri: 8,167 meters.
- Manaslu: 8,163 meters.
- Annapurna I: 8,091 meters.
- Other notable peaks include Ama Dablam, Barun Tse (7,152 m), and numerous others.

--- 8. MAJOR RIVERS AND LAKES ---

**Major River Systems:**
Nepal has four major river systems:
- Koshi River System: In the east.
- Gandaki River System: In the central region.
- Karnali River System: In the west.
- Mahakali River System: In the far west.

Of the 6,000 rivers flowing in the country, the Mahakali, Karnali, Narayani, and Koshi Rivers are fed with snow and rain water originating in the Himalaya and the Tibetan plateau. Other notable rivers include the Mechi, Kankai, Troyuga, Kamala, Bagmati, Banganga, Tinau, Rapti, Babai, and Mohan.

**Major Lakes:**
- Rara Lake: The largest lake in Nepal, located in Rara National Park.
- Phewa Lake: In Pokhara. Famous for boating with views of the Annapurna mountains.
- Begnas Lake: In Pokhara. A quieter alternative to Phewa Lake.
- Rupa Lake: In Pokhara. Smaller body of water split by a dramatic suspension bridge.
- Tilicho Lake: A high-altitude lake in the Annapurna region.
- Phoksundo Lake: In Shey Phoksundo National Park.
- Gosaikunda: A sacred alpine lake.
- Ghodaghodi Lake: A Ramsar site in the Terai.
- Ranipokhari: A historic pond in Kathmandu.
